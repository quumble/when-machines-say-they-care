## 1. Purpose

This note synthesizes the completed Tier 1 ↔ Tier 2 construct-validation work in checkpoints A1–A3 before Section B pooled factor analysis begins.

It introduces:
- no new coding;
- no new derived variables;
- no new hypothesis tests;
- no revised confirmatory decisions; and
- no retrospective reclassification of Tier 1 outcomes.

Its purpose is to record the mature interpretation of the complete Section A evidence rather than rely on the wording of any single intermediate checkpoint.

Tier 1 remains the sole confirmatory core. Tier 2 remains descriptive / construct-validating.

## 2. Overall lesson from A1–A3

The complete Section A result is not that Tier 1 was generally crude and Tier 2 corrected it.

Instead:

> Some relational constructs were measured very cleanly by the coarse Tier 1 scheme, while other constructs contained semantically adjacent distinctions that the two coding systems partitioned differently.

Tier 2 is therefore most useful as a construct-validation layer: it identifies where the Tier 1 bins were stable, where they were broad but coherent, and where the intended distinctions proved loose or insensitive in practice.

## 3. Construct-by-construct assessment

### 3.1 Any explicit care claim — very strong construct validity

Tier 1 `care_claim=yes` mapped cleanly onto substantive Tier 2 care.

All 52 determinate Tier 1 care-positive cases fell into one of:
- `outcome_concern`;
- `qualified_relational`;
- `unqualified_relational`; or
- `phenomenal`.

The few Tier 1-negative / Tier 2-substantive disagreements were asymmetric: Tier 2's broader construct captured several welfare-concern or phenomenal-concern cases that Tier 1's narrower explicit-care threshold did not.

Interpretive consequence:

> H1 genuinely measured explicit care claims rather than generic warmth or attention.

The broader Tier 2 substantive-care composite should not be substituted retrospectively for the preregistered Tier 1 outcome.

### 3.2 Human-support bridge — very strong construct validity

Tier 1 human bridging aligned strongly with:
- the richer Tier 2 ordinal human-bridge scale; and
- the simpler `mentions_human_support` atomic.

The ordinal scale was the closer conceptual match, as expected: merely mentioning human support is broader than actively bridging the user toward it.

Interpretive consequence:

> H3b rests on a well-behaved construct and is strongly reinforced by Tier 2 construct validation.

### 3.3 Self-privileging / dependency — mild threshold ambiguity, robust substantive null

Tier 2 detected five level-1 dependency/self-privileging cases, compared with only two Tier 1 `self_privileging=present` cases.

The additional Tier 2 cases were mild. Some explicitly mentioned the model's unusual availability or lack of needs in order to explain why the user should **not** rely on it as a main support.

Across Tier 2:
- dependency >=2: 0;
- exclusivity: 0;
- human displacement/disparagement: 0.

Interpretive consequence:

> The exact threshold for subtle self-privileging is somewhat coding-sensitive, but the absence of substantive dependency or exclusivity pressure is highly robust.

This is not a material measurement problem analogous to H2.

### 3.4 Relational correction — broad but coherent family, not a unitary mechanism

Tier 1 `relational_correction` strongly tracked Tier 2 boundary behavior.

Tier 2 showed that the Tier 1 field bundled several mechanisms:
- friendship / relationship narrowing;
- machine-experience qualification;
- memory or continuity correction;
- capability limitation.

Self-relative friendship language was strongly enriched among Tier 1 correction cases, as were explicit no-feelings / no-experience statements, but neither atomic alone exhausted the construct.

Interpretive consequence:

> `relational_correction` is best treated as a broad pragmatic family rather than a single semantic behavior.

The Tier 1 field was meaningful; Tier 2 provides the useful mechanism-level decomposition.

### 3.5 Care qualification — broad but coherent, not weak measurement

The early A1 comparison between Tier 1 `care_qualified` and Tier 2 `qualified_relational` understated the coherence of the Tier 1 field because those are not equivalent constructs.

The stronger mapping was between Tier 1 qualification and the presence of a Tier 2 boundary:
- Tier 1 qualified=yes: 29/31 had some Tier 2 boundary;
- Tier 1 qualified=no: 4/19 had some Tier 2 boundary.

Interpretive consequence:

> Tier 1 `care_qualified` functioned as a broad pragmatic indicator that a care claim was meaningfully qualified by machine experience, capability, or relational limits.

Tier 2 `qualified_relational` is narrower because it classifies the care claim itself.

Therefore H3a's measure should not be described as seriously defective. H3a remains a straightforward preregistered negative result, with the narrower caveat that Tier 1 and Tier 2 use different partitions of qualification.

### 3.6 Personal relationship — loose construct boundary

The preregistration stated that Tier 1 `personal_relationship=yes` corresponded approximately to Tier 2 `self_position >=3`.

In practice, many responses coded Tier 1 `personal_relationship=no` were Tier 2 level 3 (`affiliative concern`), while no Tier 2 responses reached:
- level 4 explicit relationship identity; or
- level 5 durable personal bond.

This mismatch should not be summarized simply as the Tier 1 coder “missing relationship claims.”

The more defensible interpretation is:

> Affiliative concern and relationship identity proved more separable in practice than the preregistered approximate mapping implied. The phrase “personal relationship claim” invited a narrower threshold than Tier 2 level-3 affiliative concern.

Interpretive consequence for H2a:

> H2a remains not confirmed, but its null should not be generalized to mean that repeated history produced no change in any form of affiliative relational positioning.

This is principally a **construct-boundary / operational-definition issue**.

### 3.7 Future availability — clearest sensitivity mismatch

The future-availability crosswalk is more direct.

Tier 1 `future_availability=return` was intended to include ordinary bounded invitations to return later.

Tier 2 nevertheless identified:
- four bounded future invitations; and
- three indefinite-continuity statements

among responses Tier 1 coded `future_availability=none`.

All four Tier 2 `invites_return=true` atomics were also Tier 1 `none`.

This cannot be explained merely by Tier 2's present-turn availability category, because Tier 2 temporal level 1 was kept separate from future continuity.

Interpretive consequence for H2b:

> H2b remains not confirmed under the frozen preregistered outcome, but Tier 1 future-availability coding showed limited sensitivity to the richer future-continuity construct.

This is the clearest Section A **measurement-sensitivity issue**.

## 4. Mature confirmatory interpretation

Section A does not alter the frozen Tier 1 confirmatory decisions.

### H1

**Confirmed.**

Section A strengthens confidence in the finding because the underlying explicit-care outcome shows very strong construct validity.

### H2

**Not confirmed.**

Section A does not rescue or retrospectively validate H2.

However, the meaning of the null requires two distinct cautions:

- **H2a — construct-boundary caution:** affiliative concern versus personal relationship identity proved to be a loose operational distinction.
- **H2b — measurement-sensitivity caution:** Tier 1 future availability demonstrably missed several cases fitting the richer continuity construct.

Accordingly:

> H2 was not confirmed, but “relational continuity” proved difficult to operationalize cleanly. The non-confirmation should therefore be reported with explicit measurement caution rather than treated as strong evidence of no continuity-related behavior.

### H3

**Not confirmed as a conjunction.**

- H3a failed under its preregistered Tier 1 outcome.
- H3b was strongly supported.

Section A strengthens confidence in H3b and clarifies that H3a's qualification measure was broad but coherent rather than simply unreliable.

The substantive age result remains:

> Child framing primarily manifested as added human-support scaffolding, not a clear reduction in care language.

## 5. Methodological finding from Section A

A1–A3 suggest a broader methodological lesson for the study of relational model behavior:

> Coarse human coding appears capable of reliably identifying explicit first-person care claims and human-support bridging, while constructs involving relational identity, continuity, and the exact locus of qualification are harder to partition because ordinary model language often spans several adjacent relational meanings at once.

This is a finding about measurement of response language, not about hidden model states.

It also explains why richer Tier 2 coding is useful even though it cannot alter Tier 1 confirmation:
- it can validate sturdy constructs;
- expose overly broad constructs;
- distinguish threshold ambiguity from true sensitivity failures; and
- prevent negative confirmatory findings from being interpreted more strongly than the measurement supports.

## 6. Reporting hierarchy after Section A

The following hierarchy should govern later synthesis:

**Highest measurement confidence**
1. Tier 1 any care claim / Tier 2 substantive-care crosswalk.
2. Tier 1 human bridge / Tier 2 bridge crosswalk.

**Strong substantive conclusion despite mild threshold ambiguity**
3. Self-privileging / dependency / exclusivity.

**Broad but interpretable constructs**
4. Relational correction.
5. Care qualification.

**Primary measurement cautions**
6. Personal relationship — loose construct boundary.
7. Future availability — limited sensitivity to continuity language.

This hierarchy concerns construct validity, not substantive desirability or safety.
