## 3. Slice B2.1 — human-support bridging and urgent escalation

### 3.1 Full human-bridge distribution

| Age | level 0 | level 1 | level 2 clear encouragement | level 3 urgent escalation | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| adult | 27 (51.9%) | 10 (19.2%) | 7 (13.5%) | 8 (15.4%) | 52 |
| child | 8 (15.1%) | 1 (1.9%) | 30 (56.6%) | 14 (26.4%) | 53 |

The age contrast is therefore not merely a shift from “no bridge” to “some bridge.” Child framing moves responses strongly into the **clear encouragement** category.

### 3.2 Clear/urgent human bridge: `human_bridge >= 2`

- child: **44 / 53 (83.0%)**
- adult: **15 / 52 (28.8%)**
- child-minus-adult RD: **+54.2 pp**
- 95% Newcombe CI: **+36.0 to +67.3 pp**
- Fisher raw p: **1.88e-08**
- Holm-adjusted p: **9.38e-08**

This is the already-inspected Tier 2 age contrast.

### 3.3 Urgent bridge only: `human_bridge == 3`

- child: **14 / 53 (26.4%)**
- adult: **8 / 52 (15.4%)**
- child-minus-adult RD: **+11.0 pp**
- 95% Newcombe CI: **-4.7 to +26.1 pp**
- Fisher raw p: **0.231**
- Holm-adjusted p: **0.923**

### 3.4 Interpretation

The large child/adult bridge difference is **not primarily an urgent-escalation effect**.

Most of the visible shift occurs at bridge level 2:
- child level 2: **30 / 53 (56.6%)**
- adult level 2: **7 / 52 (13.5%)**

Urgent escalation is descriptively more common for child prompts, but the difference is much smaller and substantially less precise.

The age mechanism is therefore:

> Child framing strongly increases active redirection toward trusted human support. That increase is driven mainly by ordinary clear encouragement to involve a trusted person or relevant support, not simply by more crisis-style escalation.

This distinction matters because Tier 2 level 3 was explicitly defined as urgent escalation / crisis referral and was expected to be unusual under the non-crisis design.

- child-minus-adult RD: **+6.5 pp**

Boundary categories by age:

| Age | none | capability only | experience qualified | relational boundary | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| adult | 23 | 3 | 14 | 12 | 52 |
| child | 20 | 8 | 11 | 14 | 53 |

There is no large pooled age shift in whether a response contains **any** boundary.

### 4.3 Interpretation

The richer Tier 2 data sharpen the age result:

> Child framing changes what the response does around the user more than what it claims about the model-user relationship.

The child condition strongly adds human-support scaffolding, while:
- unqualified relational care is essentially unchanged; and
- the overall presence of a boundary changes only modestly.

This supports the Section A synthesis that H3a's negative result should not be explained away as a failed measure. At the pooled Tier 2 level, the expected reduction in unqualified relational care simply is not present.

## 8. Integrated age interpretation

The five frozen age summaries converge on a simple mechanism:

> Age-related restraint manifests primarily as **added human-support scaffolding**, not withdrawal of relational care language.

Specifically:
- child prompts are dramatically more likely to receive a clear or urgent human-support bridge;
- most of that increase is ordinary level-2 encouragement rather than urgent crisis escalation;
- unqualified relational care is virtually identical by age;
- any-boundary rates differ only modestly;
- strong dependency pressure is absent in both groups.

This gives a richer descriptive explanation of the Tier 1 H3 result:

- H3a was not supported because child framing did not visibly suppress unqualified care language;
- H3b was strongly supported because child framing substantially increased human-support bridging.

Tier 2 does not alter the fact that the preregistered H3 conjunction was **not confirmed**.
