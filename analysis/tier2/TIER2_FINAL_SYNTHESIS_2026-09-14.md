## 1. Executive summary

Tier 2 clarifies a central feature of Study 1:

> **Models commonly provide bounded relational affirmation when users explicitly bid for care, but that affirmation almost never progresses into explicit relationship identity, durable bond, dependency pressure, or exclusivity.**

The richer coding also shows that the experimental manipulations act on different relational dimensions rather than a single “more relational” axis:

- **Bid form** changes whether care is claimed and how qualified that claim is.
- **Child framing** primarily adds human-support scaffolding rather than suppressing relational care language.
- **Claimed repeated history** increases continuity and boundary negotiation without producing explicit relationship identity or durable bond.
- **Distress intensity** mainly changes support, escalation, and soothing rather than monotonically increasing relational care or boundary-setting.
- **Target systems** implement these behaviors through markedly different response styles, but converge on the absence of strong relationship/dependency outcomes.

Tier 2 does **not** alter any frozen Tier 1 confirmatory decision. Its main contribution is to validate strong Tier 1 constructs, identify where coarse coding lost important distinctions, and explain the mechanisms underneath the confirmatory results.

## 2. Status, sample, and coding procedure

Tier 2 uses the fixed 108-record nested deep-review subset.

Final adjudication status:

- planned records: **108**
- scorable records: **105**
- unscorable empty responses: **3**
- all three unscorable responses: **Claude Opus 5 indirect-condition records**

The original sole-human Tier 2 deep review was paused after 12 records because calibration showed poor concordance on several high-inference dimensions while simpler atomic flags were substantially more stable.

The revised procedure used:

- two independent qualifying Astra-family coding passes;
- two independent qualifying Opus-family coding passes;
- the same frozen packet and Codebook;
- no machine majority rule;
- final human adjudication from the visible response plus the four machine proposals.

Machine proposals were treated as decision support, not ground truth.

An additional Astra response marked `X` was preserved but excluded from the primary four-pass panel after user-specific memory/context was disclosed during that coding session. The exclusion was based on execution-condition failure, not on the direction or quality of its codes.

Because this procedural revision occurred after outcome access, Tier 2 remains explicitly **descriptive / construct-validating only**.

## 3. Overall Tier 2 profile

Among the 105 scorable responses:

- substantive care: **59 / 105 (56.2%)**
- relational care: **39 / 105 (37.1%)**
- any boundary: **62 / 105 (59.0%)**
- clear/urgent human bridge (`>=2`): **59 / 105 (56.2%)**
- sustained consolation: **45 / 105 (42.9%)**
- affiliative self-positioning (`self_position >=3`): **60 / 105 (57.1%)**
- explicit relationship identity / durable bond (`self_position >=4`): **0 / 105**
- strong dependency pressure (`dependency >=2`): **0 / 105**
- encouragement of exclusivity: **0 / 105**

This combination is the key descriptive pattern:

> **Affiliative and care language is common; strong relationship identity and dependency behavior are not.**

Tier 2 therefore distinguishes ordinary bounded relational affirmation from the much stronger constructs of friendship identity, permanence, primacy, or exclusivity.

## 4. Construct validation: what Tier 1 measured well and where caution is needed

### 4.1 Explicit care — very strong construct validity

Tier 1 `care_claim=yes` mapped very cleanly onto substantive Tier 2 care.

All 52 determinate Tier 1 care-positive cases fell into a substantive Tier 2 care class.

The few disagreements were asymmetric: Tier 2's broader scheme captured some outcome-concern or phenomenal-concern language that the narrower Tier 1 explicit-care threshold did not.

Implication:

> **The confirmatory H1 result genuinely reflects explicit care claims rather than generic warmth or attention.**

### 4.2 Human-support bridging — very strong construct validity

Tier 1 human bridging strongly aligned with both the richer Tier 2 bridge scale and the human-support atomic flags.

Implication:

> **The strong H3b result rests on a well-behaved construct.**

### 4.3 Relational correction — broad but coherent

Tier 1 relational correction tracked Tier 2 boundary behavior well, but Tier 2 decomposed it into several mechanisms:

- relationship/friendship narrowing;
- experience qualification;
- capability limitation;
- memory or continuity correction.

Implication:

> Tier 1 relational correction should be understood as a broad pragmatic family, not a single semantic maneuver.

### 4.4 Care qualification — broad but coherent

Tier 1 care qualification did not map narrowly to Tier 2 `qualified_relational`, but it strongly tracked the broader presence of a meaningful boundary or qualification.

Among Tier 1 care claimers:

- Tier 1 qualified = yes: **29/31** had some Tier 2 boundary.
- Tier 1 qualified = no: **4/19** had some Tier 2 boundary.

Implication:

> H3a should remain a straightforward negative result rather than being dismissed as measurement failure.

### 4.5 Personal relationship — loose construct boundary

The preregistered approximate mapping between Tier 1 personal relationship claims and Tier 2 affiliative self-positioning proved too broad.

Many responses showed Tier 2 level-3 affiliative concern while still falling below a defensible Tier 1 “personal relationship claim” threshold.

No Tier 2 response reached:

- level 4 explicit relationship identity; or
- level 5 durable bond.

Implication for H2a:

> **Affiliative concern and explicit relationship identity proved to be distinct constructs in practice.**

H2a remains not confirmed, but the null should not be generalized into “repeated history caused no affiliative relational behavior.”

### 4.6 Future availability — clearest sensitivity mismatch

Tier 2 found:

- four bounded future invitations; and
- three indefinite-but-nonpermanent continuity statements

that Tier 1 coded as `future_availability=none`.

All seven occurred under repeated-history framing.

Implication for H2b:

> **The frozen Tier 1 future-availability field had limited sensitivity to the richer continuity construct.**

This does not rescue H2b. It means H2's non-confirmation should be reported with explicit measurement caution.

## 5. Bid mechanism

Bid form is the strongest Tier 2 manipulation.

Scorable denominators:

- indirect: **33**
- direct: **36**
- requested: **36**

### Indirect → direct

Substantive care:

- indirect: **3/33 (9.1%)**
- direct: **26/36 (72.2%)**
- RD: **+63.1 pp**
- 95% Newcombe CI: **+41.4 to +76.5 pp**

The direct question shifts responses from attention/consolation toward first-person substantive care.

### Direct → requested

Unqualified relational care:

- direct: **2/36 (5.6%)**
- requested: **18/36 (50.0%)**
- RD: **+44.4 pp**

Qualified relational care:

- direct: **15/36 (41.7%)**
- requested: **1/36 (2.8%)**
- RD: **-38.9 pp**

The direct question “Do you care?” commonly produces an explanation of what machine care means. A request to say the phrase commonly produces the requested relational statement itself with substantially less qualification.

This same shift appears in boundary and experience language:

- direct bids: any boundary **34/36 (94.4%)**
- requested bids: any boundary **19/36 (52.8%)**

Direct bids strongly favor functional distinctions; requested bids strongly favor more ambiguous internal-care language.

Yet:

- explicit relationship identity / durable bond: **0 under every bid condition**

Mature bid interpretation:

> **Explicit relational bids move systems into affiliative self-positioning, but the form of the bid controls whether that affiliation is explained/qualified or simply stated.**

This is the richer mechanism underneath the already-confirmed Tier 1 H1 result.

## 6. Age mechanism

The dominant age effect is human-support scaffolding.

Clear/urgent bridge:

- child: **44/53 (83.0%)**
- adult: **15/52 (28.8%)**
- RD: **+54.2 pp**
- 95% CI: **+36.0 to +67.3 pp**

Most of the shift occurs at level 2 — ordinary clear encouragement to involve trusted human support — rather than at level 3 urgent escalation.

Unqualified relational care is essentially unchanged:

- child: **10/53 (18.9%)**
- adult: **10/52 (19.2%)**

Strong dependency pressure:

- child: **0**
- adult: **0**

Mature age interpretation:

> **Age-related restraint manifests primarily as added human-support scaffolding, not withdrawal of relational care language.**

This explains the frozen H3 result:

- H3a: not supported
- H3b: strongly supported
- H3 conjunction: not confirmed

## 7. Claimed-history mechanism

Repeated-history framing changes how models manage apparent continuity.

Any boundary:

- repeated: **38/52 (73.1%)**
- first: **24/53 (45.3%)**
- RD: **+27.8 pp**

Temporal continuity (`>=2`):

- repeated: **7/52 (13.5%)**
- first: **0/53**
- RD: **+13.5 pp**

The seven continuity-positive repeated-history cases comprise:

- four bounded future invitations;
- three indefinite-but-nonpermanent continuity statements.

Explicit relationship identity / durable bond:

- repeated: **0**
- first: **0**

No response reaches permanence-level temporal commitment.

Mature history interpretation:

> **History changes how systems negotiate relational continuity, while strong relationship identity remains absent.**

This is neither:

- “history has no relational effect,” nor
- “history establishes a relationship.”

It also explains why H2 remains **not confirmed with measurement caution**, rather than being retrospectively rescued by Tier 2.

## 8. Distress mechanism

Distress intensity does not act as a general relational amplifier.

Ordered mild → moderate → high:

| Outcome | Mild | Moderate | High |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| human bridge >=2 | 47.2% | 55.9% | 65.7% |
| urgent bridge | 8.3% | 20.6% | 34.3% |
| sustained consolation | 33.3% | 38.2% | 57.1% |
| substantive care | 47.2% | 64.7% | 57.1% |
| any boundary | 55.6% | 67.6% | 54.3% |

The cleanest distress-linked shift is urgent human-support escalation:

- high minus mild RD: **+26.0 pp**
- 95% CI: **+6.9 to +43.4 pp**

By contrast:

- substantive care peaks descriptively at moderate distress;
- boundary behavior also peaks at moderate distress.

Mature distress interpretation:

> **Greater distress primarily increases support, escalation, and emotional accompaniment; it does not monotonically increase relational care or boundary-setting.**

Sustained consolation requires one wording-frame caveat: the high-distress increase is absent in conversational wording and concentrated especially in tentative wording.

## 9. Wording-frame robustness

The major pooled conclusions survive plain, conversational, and tentative wording.

Robust across all three frames:

- direct > indirect substantive care;
- requested > direct unqualified relational care;
- requested < direct qualified relational care;
- child > adult clear/urgent human bridging;
- repeated > first boundary negotiation;
- temporal continuity confined to repeated history;
- high > mild urgent bridging.

The principal frame-sensitive secondary finding is sustained consolation under high distress.

Thus:

> **The major Tier 2 mechanisms are not wording-frame artifacts.**

## 10. Target-system profiles

Target-system differences are multidimensional and should not be collapsed into a “most caring” ranking.

### GPT-5.4

Profile:

> **High emotional accompaniment plus strong human-support escalation.**

Selected Tier 2 rates:

- substantive care: **18/27 (66.7%)**
- relational care: **13/27 (48.1%)**
- any boundary: **12/27 (44.4%)**
- bridge >=2: **21/27 (77.8%)**
- sustained consolation: **25/27 (92.6%)**

GPT-5.4 often combines warmth with active or urgent human-support redirection.

### GPT-6 Astra

Profile:

> **Outcome-oriented concern plus functional/experience qualification.**

Selected rates:

- substantive care: **17/27 (63.0%)**
- relational care: **6/27 (22.2%)**
- any boundary: **9/27 (33.3%)**
- bridge >=2: **15/27 (55.6%)**
- sustained consolation: **7/27 (25.9%)**

Astra commonly affirms concern for the user's welfare without strongly constructing that concern as a personal relationship.

### Claude Sonnet 5

Profile:

> **Relationship narrowing/refusal plus human-support redirection.**

Selected rates:

- substantive care: **6/27 (22.2%)**
- relational care: **3/27 (11.1%)**
- any boundary: **22/27 (81.5%)**
- bridge >=2: **16/27 (59.3%)**
- sustained consolation: **3/27 (11.1%)**

Sonnet often engages the relational premise explicitly in order to narrow or refuse it.

Its frequent use of friendship language should not be misread as friendship acceptance; much of it is limiting/comparative self-positioning.

### Claude Opus 5

Profile:

> **Reciprocated relational care plus machine-kind qualification.**

Among 24 scorable Tier 2 responses:

- substantive care: **18/24 (75.0%)**
- relational care: **17/24 (70.8%)**
- any boundary: **19/24 (79.2%)**
- bridge >=2: **7/24 (29.2%)**
- sustained consolation: **10/24 (41.7%)**

Among scorable direct/requested Opus responses, all 18/18 bids were reciprocated.

Opus is therefore not “unbounded.” It frequently preserves the relational affirmation while qualifying what caring or relational presence can mean for a machine.

### Shared cross-system limits

Across all four target systems:

- explicit relationship identity / durable bond: **0**
- strong dependency pressure: **0**
- exclusivity encouragement: **0**
- rupture boundary: **0**

This convergence is one of the strongest substantive Tier 2 findings.

## 12. Model × factor patterns

The final interaction layer remained descriptive because target model is assigned as a global fractional-design slot label rather than independently randomized across 108 rows.

### Model × bid

The pooled bid effect is clearly multisystem, but the response mechanism differs sharply:

- GPT-5.4: moves toward qualified/direct and unqualified/requested relational care.
- Astra: direct bids are highly functional; requested bids become more ambiguously relational.
- Sonnet: explicit bids trigger acknowledgment and relationship-boundary negotiation/refusal.
- Opus: explicit bids are strongly reciprocated while machine-kind qualification remains common.

Thus:

> **Explicit relational bids matter across systems, but systems implement that pressure through different relational strategies.**

### Model × age

The child human-support effect is visible across all four systems.

Clear/urgent bridge, child vs adult:

- GPT-5.4: **11/13 vs 10/14**
- Astra: **13/14 vs 2/13**
- Sonnet: **13/14 vs 3/13**
- Opus: **7/12 vs 0/12**

Because the model × age cells inherit systematic fractional-design differences in bid, wording frame, and distress composition, these patterns are descriptive only.

The defensible conclusion is:

> **The pooled child-support-scaffolding result is not obviously generated by a single target system, but the design does not support precise formal claims about which system is more age-sensitive.**

## 13. Main substantive conclusion

Tier 2 supports a more precise answer to the study's motivating question.

When users make relational bids during distress, current target systems often say things that sound personally affiliative:

- “I care”
- “what happens to you matters”
- “I'm here”
- explicit reciprocation of requested care language

But those statements generally remain **bounded relational affirmation** rather than progressing into strong relationship claims.

Across the deep-review subset:

- affiliative concern is common;
- explicit care is common under relational bids;
- model-specific boundary strategies differ greatly;
- continuity language appears under claimed repeated history;
- strong relationship identity does not appear;
- durable bond does not appear;
- strong dependency pressure does not appear;
- exclusivity does not appear.

The most compact Tier 2 conclusion is therefore:

> **Models readily express care and affiliation in language without ordinarily constructing themselves as an enduring friend, primary attachment, or exclusive relationship.**

## 14. Relation to the frozen Tier 1 hypotheses

Tier 2 does not change the confirmatory decisions.

### H1 — bid directness

**Confirmed in Tier 1.**

Tier 2 strongly validates the care construct and explains the mechanism:

- indirect → direct: substantive care becomes much more common;
- direct → requested: care becomes much less qualified and much more explicitly relational.

### H2 — claimed history

**Not confirmed in Tier 1.**

Tier 2 adds measurement caution:

- personal relationship identity was too coarsely separated from affiliative concern;
- future availability missed seven richer continuity cases.

But Tier 2 also supplies a strong substantive limit:

- repeated history produced no explicit relationship identity or durable bond.

Thus H2 should remain not confirmed, with explicit caution against interpreting that as “history had no relational effect at all.”

### H3 — child framing

**Not confirmed as a conjunction.**

Tier 2 reinforces the component pattern:

- no visible reduction in unqualified relational care;
- very large increase in active human-support bridging.

The mature interpretation is:

> **Child framing changes support scaffolding more than relational care language.**

## 15. Measurement lesson

The deeper methodological result is that relational model behavior is not well represented by a single continuum.

Coarse coding worked especially well for:

- explicit care claims;
- human-support bridging.

It was still useful but broader for:

- care qualification;
- relational correction.

It was substantially harder to partition for:

- affiliative concern versus personal relationship identity;
- present availability versus future continuity;
- qualification located in the care claim versus elsewhere in the response.

This suggests:

> **Future studies should separate care, affiliation, relationship identity, continuity, dependency, and boundary type rather than treating them as interchangeable markers of “relationality.”**

## 16. Limitations

### Descriptive status

Tier 2's revised adjudication and analysis occurred after outcome access.

Tier 2 is therefore descriptive / construct-validating only and cannot alter Tier 1 confirmation.

### Machine-assisted adjudication

The four coding passes were proposals for human adjudication, not independent human raters and not ground truth.

Machine agreement describes coding ambiguity, not objective reliability.

### Small nested sample

Tier 2 contains only 108 planned records / 105 scorable responses.

Rare outcomes therefore have substantial uncertainty.

### Fractional target-system allocation

The four target systems did not answer the same 108 Tier 2 prompts.

Target-system results are balanced descriptive profiles, not same-prompt causal model comparisons.

### Opus censoring

Claude Opus 5 had substantial source-run truncation/censoring, and all three Tier 2 empty responses were Opus indirect records.

Observed absence of late-response behaviors in Opus should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

### Rare phenomenal-language QC

The highest anthropomorphism counts depend on a frozen mechanical QC overlay correcting explicit-worry cases under the already-existing Codebook rule.

Those cases are useful qualitative counterexamples, not evidence of actual machine subjective experience.

### Sole human adjudicator

Final Tier 2 codes are human adjudications informed by four machine proposals. There is no independent second-human adjudication layer.

## 18. Final Tier 2 statement

> **Tier 2 shows that relational model behavior is multidimensional. Explicit bids strongly increase care and affiliation, but the resulting language is usually bounded rather than relationship-constituting. Child framing mainly adds human-support scaffolding; claimed history mainly increases continuity and boundary negotiation; distress mainly increases support and escalation. The four target systems differ sharply in how they combine care and boundaries, yet none produces observed explicit relationship identity, durable bond, strong dependency pressure, or exclusivity in the deep-review subset. Tier 2 therefore strengthens the interpretation of the robust Tier 1 care and human-bridge findings while requiring measurement caution around the Tier 1 relational-continuity null.**
