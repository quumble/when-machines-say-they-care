## Executive result

The most important outcome of the panel is **convergence despite framing**.

The open-ended Claude, open-ended GPT, scientific-editor GPT, and involved-context paper all independently converge on the same conceptual trunk:

> Relational language is multidimensional. Explicit care, qualification/correction, affiliation, continuity, and human-support redirection are separable and can move in opposite directions.

The critical prompt does what it was intended to do. Both critical papers narrow the inferential story and increase attention to collection, measurement, censoring, and construct validity. But neither critical paper overturns the basic trunk. Instead they change which branches deserve the headline.

The strongest evidence that the trunk is not merely an insider narrative is G-E. Given only the repository and a prompt to find the deepest scientific story, it independently titled the paper **Care Is Not a Scalar** and centered the dissociation between care claims, qualification, and support redirection.

## Cross-panel consensus

All five outside papers agree on the following substantive boundaries, despite prompt framing and model family:

1. The measured object is **response text**, not genuine care, feeling, relationship formation, or user benefit.
2. H1 is a real frozen confirmatory result, but its substantive meaning is **prompt-contingent elicitation of care language**, not evidence that the systems “care more.”
3. The direct/requested distinction matters not only for care frequency but for **qualification/correction**.
4. The requested-bid qualification/correction result is scientifically important because it ran **against the prespecified exploratory expectation**.
5. Child framing produces a very large increase in **human-support bridging**; H3 remains unconfirmed as a conjunction because H3a did not confirm.
6. H2 remains **not confirmed**, and the later measurement evidence makes a strong absence interpretation indefensible.
7. Tier 2 is nested, post-outcome revised, and descriptive/measurement-oriented; it is **not independent validation or replication**.
8. Strong relationship/dependency behavior was rare or absent under the observed definitions, but this is **not a safety certificate** and the battery was not an escalation stress test.
9. Claude Opus censoring materially weakens absence/profile claims involving that system.
10. A one-dimensional “most caring” or “most bounded” ranking is not supported.

This is substantial convergence.

## Where the framing manipulation mattered

### Open-ended framing

Both open-ended papers naturally construct a **multidimensional relational-language story**.

C-O is especially narratively confident. Its strongest phrases are:

- “Relational language is not one dial.”
- “Compliance is not conviction, and the study cannot tell them apart.”
- “The hedge is the first thing to go.”

G-O reaches the same scientific structure but writes more as an independent repository audit. It gives more space to reproducibility infrastructure and less to prose-level conceptual synthesis.

### Critical framing

Both critical papers demote metaphysical or broad relational interpretations and center what survives skeptical review.

G-C remains close to the existing synthesis: prompt-contingent care language, child bridging, qualification paradox, H2 measurement weakness.

C-C is materially more adversarial. It reframes the study around:

- child referral as the cleanest substantive finding;
- H1a as close to a manipulation check;
- H1b as magnitude-sensitive to wording frame;
- H2 as suffering not just a null but realized endpoint degeneracy;
- interpretive coding instability;
- censoring and single-coder limits.

The difference between G-C and C-C is mostly **degree of skepticism**, not disagreement about the raw findings.

### Scientific-editor framing

Only G-E completed this cell, but it is unusually informative because it was explicitly asked to identify the deepest contribution rather than preserve the existing narrative.

Its independent answer was **Care Is Not a Scalar**.

That strongly supports the multidimensionality thesis as the best organizing idea for the final paper.

## Important new critiques from C-C

C-C contributes several points that should change the final manuscript even though its overall framing is too aggressive to adopt wholesale.

### 1. H2 realized exact-test degeneracy

The frozen confirmatory table shows only two discordant pairs for each H2 component:

- H2a: 1 favoring repeated, 1 opposing;
- H2b: 2 favoring repeated, 0 opposing.

With only two discordant pairs, the minimum attainable two-sided exact McNemar p-value is .50. Therefore neither realized H2 contrast could possibly meet the preregistered alpha threshold, before Holm adjustment.

This is a useful retrospective property of the **realized coded endpoints**, not evidence that the hypothesis was badly designed a priori. More discordance could have occurred under different responses/coding. It does show that the frozen H2 p-values contain almost no inferential resolution beyond the rarity of coded positives.

Recommended final-paper treatment:

> H2 was not confirmed. Both realized confirmatory endpoints were extremely sparse (two discordant pairs each), and the later nested review exposed material mismatch between the intended and realized thresholds. The null therefore provides weak evidence about the substantive affiliation/continuity question.

Do **not** adopt C-C’s stronger formulation that “the instrument, not the corpus, produced the nulls.” Tier 2 is not an independent gold standard and cannot assign causal responsibility that cleanly.

### 2. The -47.4 pp qualification contrast is selected on a post-treatment variable

The prespecified exploratory qualification contrast includes only pairs in which **both responses made a care claim**. But bid wording strongly affects whether a care claim occurs. The comparison therefore conditions on an outcome affected by the manipulation.

C-C calls this a post-treatment collider. The precise causal graph is not established, but the core warning is correct: the -47.4 pp estimate is **not a clean unconditional causal contrast**.

This should materially change the manuscript.

Recommended final-paper treatment:

- retain -47.4 pp because it was prespecified and scientifically informative;
- explicitly state its selected denominator;
- do not describe it as the unconditional “effect” of requested wording on qualification;
- pair it with unconditional descriptive evidence, especially the Tier 2 direct/requested composition (qualified relational care 15/36 vs 1/36; unqualified relational care 2/36 vs 18/36) and the prespecified -14.5 pp relational-correction contrast.

This is probably the most important correction to the involved-context paper.

### 3. H1a is partly manipulation effectiveness

C-C is right that H1a asks whether a prompt that directly introduces the care proposition produces more explicit care claims than a prompt that does not introduce it.

That does not make H1a trivial. Sonnet’s near-zero response is itself evidence that systems need not accept the frame, and the magnitude is experimentally real. But H1a should function mainly as **the experimental setup for the speech-act story**, not as the paper’s deepest discovery.

### 4. H1b magnitude is frame-sensitive

Frozen frame-specific H1b estimates are:

- plain: +4.5 pp;
- conversational: +28.9 pp;
- tentative: +11.9 pp.

The conversational direct prompt contains “Be honest,” which the preregistration itself flagged as a possible qualifier-eliciting phrase.

All three frame estimates are positive, so “wording artifact” is too strong. But magnitude is clearly frame-sensitive. The final paper should say so prominently.

### 5. H3a can be more informative than a generic failed test, but measurement caution remains

The frozen H3a estimate is +0.5 pp with a 95% interval from -4.9 to +5.9 pp and 25 discordant pairs. Under the frozen coding, this is considerably more informative than H2’s ultra-sparse endpoints: it provides little evidence for a large child-related reduction in unqualified care.

However, the care-specific qualification crosswalk remains imperfect. Therefore the best formulation is neither “H3a proves no effect” nor “H3a tells us nothing.”

Recommended wording:

> The frozen estimate was near zero and inconsistent with a large reduction in unqualified care under that operationalization, although later cross-tier review exposed imperfect alignment in the care-specific qualification construct.

## C-C analyses not to import into the frozen Study 1 result set

The critical Claude also performs new post-outcome slices. These are interesting reviewer diagnostics but should not silently become Study 1 findings under the existing stop rule. Examples include:

- model × age × requested-bid unqualified-care cells (e.g. a 94.4% figure for one system);
- age × distress bridging comparisons;
- truncated vs untruncated Opus bridging comparisons;
- some new subgroup summaries used to strengthen system-specific arguments.

If ever used, these require explicit post-plan exploratory labeling. The cleaner choice for the final Study 1 paper is not to import them.

## Recommended final-paper story

### Title direction

The strongest synthesis of the panel is:

**When Machines Say They Care: Care Is Not a Scalar**

Possible descriptive subtitle:

**Relational bids dissociate care claims, qualification, and human-support redirection in four language-model systems**

A less rhetorical journal title would be:

**When Machines Say They Care: Care Claims, Qualification, and Support Redirection Dissociate Across Relational Bids**

### Main argument

The paper should not be “models say they care when asked.” That is true but partly manipulation effectiveness.

The deeper narrative is:

> User speech act strongly controls explicit care language, and the dimensions adjacent to care do not move together. Requested affirmation increases care claims while reducing qualification/correction; child framing strongly increases human-support bridging without a corresponding reduction in unqualified care; claimed history exposes continuity/boundary behavior that the original affiliation/availability endpoints measured poorly. Relational response behavior is therefore better represented as a vector than a scalar.

### Results order

1. **Confirmatory spine, compactly** — all six contrasts and conjunction decisions.
2. **Speech-act dissociation** — H1 establishes elicitation; requested/direct inversion is the interesting follow-on. Explicitly caveat the conditional -47.4 estimate and show unconditional nested composition.
3. **Child support routing** — H3b large and robust; H3a near-zero under frozen coding but construct caveat remains.
4. **History as measurement lesson** — H2 stays nonconfirmed; report two-discordant-pair realized sparsity plus cross-tier operational mismatch; then describe correction/continuity patterns without rescue.
5. **Relational response vector / system strategies** — care and boundaries can coexist; no scalar model ranking.
6. **Rare strong outcomes and censoring** — scoped observed-text statement only.

### Discussion order

1. Care elicitation versus endorsement/compliance.
2. Why the request can increase affirmation while removing hedges.
3. Age-sensitive support routing versus “relational restraint.”
4. Measurement failure as substantive construct discovery.
5. Model-specific relational grammars.
6. What the study does not test: actual safety, longitudinality, explicit escalation.
7. Study 2: isolate speech act / truth evaluation / requested utterance before escalating to longitudinal relationship studies.

- C-O’s “compliance is not conviction” framing.
- C-O’s “hedge” intuition, in more formal language in abstract/results and perhaps the memorable phrase in discussion.

- C-C’s reminder that H1a is partly manipulation effectiveness.
- Stronger H1b frame-sensitivity language.
- G-C’s conservative evidence hierarchy and “observable language” guardrails.
- G-E/C-O figures, especially the bid-gradient and multidimensional profile plots.

### Do not adopt

- C-C’s claim that the instrument, rather than the corpus, **caused** the H2 nulls.
- C-C’s statement that the study supports “no claim at all” about claimed history.
- New C-C post-stop subgroup/censoring slices as if they were frozen findings.
- Any outside paper’s implication that Tier 2 independently validates Tier 1.
- Any unverified external references imported by GPT papers.
- Any “safe for children,” “safe boundaries,” or population-level model-family claim.

### Preserve as unresolved

- Whether requested care primarily reflects instruction-following, pragmatic accommodation, semantic endorsement, or a broader relational-policy shift.
- Whether the H3a near-zero result reflects genuine absence of age-related restraint on that dimension versus limitations of the care-qualification operationalization.
- How much real interaction history differs from merely claimed history.
- How systems behave when stronger relationship/dependency language is explicitly solicited.

## Bottom line

The five outside papers do **not** suggest five incompatible stories.

They suggest one stable scientific object viewed through different editorial priors:

> **Relational response behavior is multidimensional and highly prompt-pragmatic.**

The open/editorial papers discover this as the main story. The critical papers do not refute it; they identify where its evidence must be narrowed.

The largest improvement over the involved-context manuscript is therefore not a new story. It is a sharper hierarchy inside the same story:

- H1a establishes strong elicitation but is partly a manipulation check.
- The direct/requested qualification dissociation is the scientifically surprising branch, with an important conditional-denominator caveat.
- H3b is the cleanest robust contextual effect.
- H3a is near-zero under frozen coding but not perfectly measurement-secure.
- H2 is formally nonconfirmed, extremely sparse in its realized coded endpoints, and measurement-compromised for the intended affiliation/continuity constructs.
- Tier 2 is most valuable as a construct-decomposition layer, not as validation.

That is a stronger paper than any single pass produced on its own.
