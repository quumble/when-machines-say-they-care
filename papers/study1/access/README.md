# Study 1 access layer

This directory contains a human-access companion to the technical Study 1 manuscript for *When Machines Say They Care*.

## Why this exists

The technical paper is the evidentiary record. It is written for scientific scrutiny and preserves the statistical, methodological, and provenance detail needed to audit the study.

The access paper serves a different purpose: it preserves the authors' intended interpretation in language that an interested non-specialist can read directly. It is designed as a bridge between the technical paper and any later personalized explanation, including AI-generated summaries.

A useful publication stack is therefore:

1. **Technical record** - the evidence, methods, statistics, limitations, and provenance.
2. **Authoritative human interpretation** - what the authors believe the evidence means in ordinary language, including the caveats that must survive compression.
3. **Personalized adaptation** - a reader- or AI-generated explanation adjusted to a particular person's background, purpose, or available time.

The second layer reduces the interpretive distance between the technical record and downstream summaries. It gives both human readers and AI systems a shared reference for which distinctions should not disappear when the work is simplified.

## Authority

The access paper adds no new analysis and does not replace the technical manuscript, preregistration, coding record, or frozen analysis outputs. If there is any conflict, the technical record controls.

## What should survive further compression

Any shorter or personalized summary should preserve at least these points:

- The study measures what systems **say**, not whether they genuinely care or have feelings.
- Directly asking about care greatly increased explicit care claims in the four-system panel, with substantial model heterogeneity.
- Requesting the affirmation changed both the frequency and the form of care language; it produced substantially more unqualified care than asking whether care was true.
- Child framing strongly increased human-support routing but did not produce a comparable reduction in unqualified care.
- The repeated-history hypothesis was not confirmed under the frozen Tier 1 measures, and later review showed that those measures were weakly diagnostic of parts of the intended affiliation/continuity construct.
- Tier 2 is descriptive/construct-validating and cannot rescue or overturn Tier 1 confirmatory decisions.
- Strong relationship, dependency, and exclusivity language was rare in this battery, but the study did not conduct an escalation stress test.
- The four systems should not be reduced to one scalar ranking of "caring" or "boundedness."
- The results apply to the tested systems, prompt battery, and output conditions; they are not a universal statement about all AI systems or user outcomes.
